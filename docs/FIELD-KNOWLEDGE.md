# HARDBOND field knowledge and product direction

Source: owner's “Master Work Context, Field Operations Manual, Project History & AI Assistant Instructions”, supplied October 23, 2026. This is a curated extraction for HARDBOND Command. The attachment remains the original source; this file does not establish current product specifications, inventory, attendance or active projects.

## What the app should optimize

Reduce what Aidan must remember. Show the next action, put the right information beside the job, and capture actual results once. Daily Sheets remain the main record. Keep phone entry short; reveal optional measurement and reference details on demand. Quality, safe execution and protecting the next phase govern the sequence.

The useful daily loop:

1. Before mobilizing: confirm scope, address, access, shutdown/reopening restrictions, target, attending crew, actual stock and equipment.
2. Divide the work: name a responsible person, first tasks, equipment and the likely bottleneck. More workers do not fix a single-bucket or single-machine constraint.
3. Before changing phase: check the specified substrate condition, repairs, cleanliness, product requirements and relevant cure/recoat window.
4. Before leaving: record output, crew intervals, actual consumption, delay cause, unresolved issues, cleaned equipment and next-day needs.
5. Learn from reviewed records with matching area/time measurements; preserve missing information as unknown.

## Implemented from this material

- Job-linked field plans with ordered, repeatable phases; named responsibility; target and crew assignments; equipment/material staging notes; prerequisites; technical source/acceptance notes; phase checks; status; hold reason and handover.
- Phase prompts for setup, preparation, repairs, primer/moisture barrier, base/broadcast, finish and cure/handover. Add only applicable phases and change the order to the specified system. These are planning prompts, not approved installation procedures.
- The last job Daily Sheet's handover and issues appear alongside the plan, visibly labeled when still a draft.
- Recorded stock gaps appear alongside the plan. This comparison does not allocate or reserve inventory and does not replace Aidan's physical check.
- Optional single-phase production measurement on Daily Sheets. A matching-work confirmation is required before analysis uses it. Crew clocks must all be complete for a labor rate; travel remains separate. Separate phases should use separate sheets.
- Analysis shows individual observations by job, phase and date. It does not combine unlike work into a promised benchmark or invent profit figures.
- Crew-name suggestions derive from entered records rather than treating a historical roster as today's crew.
- Inventory can retain a product source, checked date, coverage per counting unit, coverage conditions and handling notes. The optional job material planner requires those details and an explicit applicability confirmation, then records floor/wall areas, coats, allowance, rounding and a source snapshot with the job requirement. It does not reserve or consume stock.
- Field plans open a Daily Sheet for the selected job and display the planned phase and previous handover as reference, without recording planned work as completed work.
- The overview offers up to three concrete next steps, including job holds, overdue actions and missing details. Phase forms collapse to keep the sequence readable on a phone.
- Offline shell registration tolerates a notification-provider script failure, so optional external notification availability does not disable local record access.

Existing job records and backups remain readable. New records carry validated optional planning/measurement fields. Inventory consumption still occurs through reviewed Daily Sheets, with the existing transactional correction and recount protections.

## Equipment reference — availability must be checked

Historical equipment: TM-LION; 24-inch grinder; 7-inch hand grinders; PCD; metal-bond and resin-bond diamonds; multiple industrial vacuums; mixers; clean separate buckets; squeegees, rollers, brushes and handles; scrapers; Kings Champion 12,500-watt generator. Historical grits are not a universal tooling sequence. Select the required surface result first, then the tooling, extraction and power arrangement.

## Lessons worth retaining

- Car wash: about 2,300 sq ft floor and 400–500 sq ft walls. Two workers reported approximately 10:00–17:30 grinding, with a later combined preparation day through 21:45 and about 1.5 hours of setup/cleanup. Area and phase time boundaries overlap or are incomplete. Do not derive a definitive production benchmark from these reports.
- Walls-first was a preference for a planned four-person coating day. Evaluate transition details, access, timing and contamination for each actual system; it is not a universal sequence.
- Metallic job: about 580 sq ft, a historical two-kit/broadcast/two-kit sequence and water-mark-like appearance. Exact products and executed steps need confirmation. The restriction on diamonds belonged to that finish situation.
- Colour batching: four LGR kits, two clear kits, three black pigments and one spare bucket were a historical constraint. Verify compatibility, pigment loading, homogenization and clean containers before planning consistent batches.
- Exposed reinforcement: determine extent, soundness, corrosion and repair requirements before selecting a filler or coating. Decorative aggregate is not automatically a suitable repair system.
- Stone contamination: the PP-S85/limestone/Venetian-finish incident illustrates the need to identify the finish and test a restoration method before escalating solvents or abrasion.
- Floor leveling: use a repeatable datum/grid and real depths before calculating volume or bags. A visual check or improvised straightedge is not a complete elevation survey.

## Useful next improvements, not implemented yet

1. Extend the source-backed material planner with manufacturer document verification, component/volume conversions and multiple coverage rates for different surfaces. The current planner uses one documented area-per-counting-unit rate per product; it does not derive film thickness, approve compatibility, or fetch/verify documents automatically.
2. Equipment condition and job reservations: identify simultaneous demand, damaged/missing equipment, clean bucket availability, material expiry and storage conditions. Existing material lists are not enforced reservations.
3. Job-specific phase timing: planned/actual timestamps, dependencies and explicit technical hold releases; no automatic cure-release from a generic timer.
4. Photo evidence and inspection history, with practical mobile capture and a storage/backup strategy.
5. Comparable phase benchmarks with context such as system, repair extent, equipment, conditions, setup/cleanup and rework. Add costing only when actual rates/costs exist.
6. Short company-approved procedures and supervised practical training. Quiz completion alone cannot qualify someone to use hazardous equipment.

Keep notifications contextual and user-controlled. Do not change schedules, send messages or populate live job/stock records from historical notes. TimeTree remains the team schedule; Jobber/WhatsApp/Sheets/Notion integrations are unconfirmed. DC/MC/AC abbreviations need clarification before use. Marketing metrics, career research and course suggestions were omitted from the operational app because they do not help today's field workflow.
